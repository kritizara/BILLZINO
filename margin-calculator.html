What margin means

Margin is the share of the selling price that stays with you after the direct cost. A 100 sale with a 60 cost leaves 40 profit, which is a 40% margin. The same numbers are a 66.7% markup.

Why margin, not markup, decides viability

Rent, salaries, software and tax are paid from margin, not from markup. A business that prices on markup alone can grow revenue while losing money. Track margin per product or service, not only for the company as a whole.

Pricing to a target margin

To hit a margin target, divide the cost by (1 − target). For a 40% margin on a 60 cost: 60 ÷ 0.6 = 100. Do not add 40% to the cost — that gives 84, a 28.6% margin.

Check it on the invoice

When you send the invoice, the customer sees the price, not your cost. Keep a separate sheet with cost and margin per line so you can review it after the job. If a discount is requested, run it through the discount calculator before accepting.

Margin and markup formulas

Margin is measured against the selling price, markup against the cost. The same deal always has a higher markup than margin.

A frequent mistake is adding 40% markup when you meant a 40% margin: 60 × 1.40 = 84, which is only a 28.6% margin ((84 − 60) ÷ 84). If you are pricing for a margin, divide the cost by 1 minus the margin.

Frequently asked questions

What is a good profit margin?

It depends on the industry. Retail often runs 5–10%, services 20–50%, software much higher. Compare with your own sector, not with a generic number.

How do I calculate margin from cost and price?

(price − cost) ÷ price × 100. A 100 price with a 60 cost is a 40% margin.

What is the difference between gross and net margin?

Gross margin counts only direct costs; net margin also subtracts overheads, salaries and tax.

Can margin be negative?

Yes, when the price is below the cost. That is a loss on every sale.

How do I increase margin without raising prices?

Reduce the direct cost, bundle a higher-margin item, or remove a discount.

What is the profit margin formula?

Profit margin % = (revenue − cost) ÷ revenue × 100. A product sold for 100 that costs 80 has a 20% margin. Markup vs margin: markup is measured against cost, margin against the selling price.
