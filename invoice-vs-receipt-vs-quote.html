Invoice vs receipt vs quote

These documents look similar but do different jobs. A quote offers a price, an invoice asks for payment, and a receipt confirms it. Here is how to tell them apart.

The short answer

A quote says what something will cost, before the work. An invoice asks the customer to pay, after or at delivery. A receipt proves the customer has paid. In order: quote → invoice → receipt.

Quote vs estimate

A quote is a fixed price offer. An estimate is an approximation that may change if the work turns out bigger or smaller. Use a quote when scope is clear and an estimate when it isn't.

Invoice vs tax invoice

A tax invoice is an invoice that shows tax (VAT, GST or sales tax) as a separate line. Businesses that charge tax often must issue one. Requirements vary by country, so check your local rules.

How the three work together

  1. Quote — you send a price. The client says yes.
  2. Invoice — you do the work and bill the client.
  3. Receipt — the client pays and you confirm it.

Example: one job, three documents

  1. Quote Q-014: 1,500 for a logo and brand sheet, valid for 30 days.
  2. Invoice INV-0042: after delivery, 1,500 due in 14 days.
  3. Receipt R-0042: sent when the 1,500 arrives, referencing INV-0042.

Each document keeps the same client details and a reference to the one before it, which makes the whole job easy to follow later.

Common confusions, cleared up

  • Invoice vs bill: they are the same document seen from two sides — the seller sends an invoice, the buyer records it as a bill.
  • Quote vs estimate: a quote is a fixed price you stand behind; an estimate is your best guess and may change.
  • Paid invoice vs receipt: a receipt is the proof of payment. Marking an invoice “paid” is useful internally, but many clients still ask for a separate receipt.
  • Proforma vs invoice: a proforma announces an amount before the sale is final and is not normally recorded as revenue.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between an invoice and a receipt?

An invoice asks the customer to pay. A receipt confirms the customer has already paid.

What is the difference between a quote and an invoice?

A quote is an offer made before the work. An invoice is a request for payment sent after or at delivery.

Is an estimate the same as a quote?

No. A quote is a fixed price. An estimate is approximate and can change.

Do I need all three documents?

Not always. Many small businesses use only an invoice, and add a quote for larger jobs and a receipt when a customer needs proof of payment.
